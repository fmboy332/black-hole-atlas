Strange Worlds

The universe builds planets that make Earth look boring. Every number here comes from the NASA Exoplanet Archive (planetary systems composite table) or NASA's exoplanet catalogue, checked 4 Nov 2026. Confidence tags (established, interpretation) come from our science checker's notes. Light-year figures are converted from the Archive's parsecs.

TRAPPIST-1 system

Confidence: established

Seven planets around one tiny red star, about 40.5 light years away. TRAPPIST-1 e goes around its star in just 6.1 days. A full 'year' shorter than a week, and you'd still be late for it.

Source

55 Cancri e

Confidence: established

NASA describes it as covered in a global ocean of lava with sparkling skies interpretation (a model interpretation, not a direct observation). Its year lasts about 0.74 days, so birthdays come roughly every 18 hours. Equilibrium temperature around 1,958 K.

Source

HD 189733 b

Confidence: interpretation

A gorgeous cobalt-blue planet with a terrible personality: NASA says its clouds are laced with glass in a blow-torched atmosphere interpretation (the blue colour is measured; the glass is a model interpretation). About 64.5 light years away.

Source

WASP-12 b

Confidence: established

The 'doomed planet': it orbits so close to its star that its star stretches it into an egg shape, and it is slowly spiralling inward, predicted to be destroyed in roughly a few million years. One orbit takes about 1.1 days.

Source

KELT-9 b

Confidence: established

So hot (equilibrium temperature about 3,921 K) that NASA says the heat tears molecules apart on its dayside. Sunscreen is not going to cut it.

Source

Kepler-452 b

Confidence: interpretation

NASA's 'Earth-cousin': it orbits a Sun-like star in the habitable zone. About 1.63 times Earth's radius, which means it may not be rocky at all, with a year of about 385 days. Roughly 1,800 light years away.

Source

Proxima Centauri b

Confidence: established

Our nearest known exoplanet neighbour, about 4.2 light years away. A minimum mass of about 1.06 Earths and an 11.2-day year.

Source

Kepler-16 b

Confidence: established

A real two-sun planet: the Archive lists two stars in its system. It loops around both of them every ~229 days.

Source

PSR B1257+12 planets

Confidence: established

Three planets orbiting a pulsar, the spinning core left after a star exploded. Two of them were discovered in 1992. Living next door to a cosmic lighthouse? Bold choice.

Source
