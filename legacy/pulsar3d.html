Illustration, not a photograph. Nobody can see a pulsar like this. The beams, field lines, glow and colours are artistic. The star is drawn far bigger than the scene would really allow, and the light cylinder is not to scale (it is compressed so it fits on screen). The pulse timing, the "does Earth see it?" check and the numbers do come from the real geometry and standard formulas.

The math behind a pulsar

What you are looking at

White-blue ball: the neutron star. Purple loops: magnetic field lines of a simple dipole, lined up with the magnetic axis, which is tilted (α) from the spin axis (dashed line), so the whole field wobbles as the star turns. Blue cones: the two beams from the magnetic poles. Faint cylinder: the light cylinder, where anything turning with the star would have to move at light speed. Blue dot: Earth's direction (angle ζ from the spin axis). It flashes when a beam sweeps over it, in step with the pulse strip below the view.

For fast pulsars the on-screen spin is slowed to at most 1.5 turns per second so you can follow it; the sound and the numbers use the true period. Real pulsars are mostly seen as radio pulses, and the sound here is a sonification. The lighthouse model is well established, but exactly how the radio emission is made is still debated.
