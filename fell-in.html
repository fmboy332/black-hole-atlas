What if you fell in? 🕳️

Pick a black hole, pick your height, and find out where you turn into spaghetti. The physics is real. The humour is mine.

Fun setting, not medical advice. The point is where stretching beats your body. Feet-first, because why not.

Formulas: tidal stretch across your body is about 2GML / r³. Event horizon radius is 2GM / c². The distance where the stretch hits your limit is r = (2GML / a)^(1/3). Treated as Newtonian, which is fine for the idea but only approximate very close to the hole.

Time dilation: your friend's slow clock ⏳

Your friend hovers at a fixed distance from the black hole while you watch from far away. Slide them closer and see how slowly their clock ticks. (Hovering that close would need an absurd rocket, so this is a thought experiment.)

Formula for a hovering clock: dτ/dt = √(1 − rs/r), with rs = 2GM/c². Standard Schwarzschild (non-spinning) result. It gets to zero only at the horizon itself. Their own clock feels totally normal to them.

The twist

Bigger black hole, gentler horizon. Stretching at the horizon falls as 1/M². Fall into a supermassive one and you'd cross the horizon feeling basically nothing. The bad part comes later, deeper inside.
A clock hovering close to the horizon runs slow as seen from far away. Falling in feels normal to you, but anyone watching from far away would see you slow down and freeze at the edge.
Nobody has ever been to one, so "what you'd see inside" is theory. Anyone telling you for certain is selling something.
