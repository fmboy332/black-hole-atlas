Glossary

Plain-language definitions of space words.

Black hole
A region where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape.
Event horizon
The boundary of no return around a black hole.
Schwarzschild radius
The horizon radius of a non-spinning black hole, 2GM/c², about 2.95 km per solar mass.
Singularity
The predicted point of infinite density at the center. Our physics likely breaks down there.
Accretion disk
A hot, glowing disk of gas spiraling around a black hole.
ISCO
Innermost stable circular orbit. Matter closer than this spirals in.
Photon sphere
The region where light can orbit a black hole unstably.
Spin (a)
How fast a black hole rotates, as a number from 0 to 1.
Frame dragging
A spinning mass drags spacetime around with it.
Tidal force
The difference in gravity across an object, which stretches it.
Spaghettification
Being stretched into a long thin shape by tidal forces.
Supernova
The explosion of a massive star at the end of its life.
Gravitational waves
Ripples in spacetime, for example from merging black holes.
Hawking radiation
Predicted faint radiation from black holes. Not yet observed.
Solar mass
The mass of our Sun, a unit for measuring stars and black holes.
Pulsar
A spinning neutron star whose beams of radiation sweep past Earth, so we see regular pulses. Try the pulsar simulator.
Neutron star
The collapsed core left by some supernovae: roughly 1.1 to 2 suns of mass in a ball about 20 to 24 km across.
Magnetar
A neutron star with an extremely strong magnetic field, roughly 10¹⁴ to 10¹⁵ gauss (about 10¹⁰ to 10¹¹ tesla).
Light cylinder
The distance from a pulsar where something spinning along with it would have to move at light speed: R = cP/2π.
Pulse profile
A graph of how a pulsar's brightness changes during one turn.
Sonification
Turning data, like radio pulses, into sound so we can hear patterns.
Eddington luminosity
The rough maximum brightness at which radiation pressure balances gravity, about 1.26×10³¹ W per solar mass.
Relativistic beaming
Matter moving near light speed looks much brighter when it comes toward you and dimmer when it moves away.
Chirp mass
A combination of two masses, (m₁m₂)^⅗/(m₁+m₂)^⅕, that sets how fast a gravitational-wave signal sweeps up in frequency.
Tidal disruption
When tidal forces tear a star apart near a black hole. See the merger simulator.
Kilonova
A glow from radioactive matter thrown out when neutron stars merge or are torn apart.
Ringdown
The last part of a merger signal, when the new black hole wobbles and settles down, giving off fading gravitational waves.
