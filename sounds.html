Sounds of Space

Quick truth first: space is a near-vacuum, so sound like you hear in air can't travel through it. (Hot gas in galaxy clusters is the rare exception, and even those waves are far too low for ears.) Everything here is one of two things:

RECORDING radio waves converted to audio: radio and plasma waves measured by a spacecraft's antenna and shifted into a pitch you can hear. Not microphone sound. SONIFICATION telescope data mapped to sound (pitch, volume, instruments) by scientists.

Volume warning: start low. Nothing autoplays.

SONIFICATION

Black hole · Perseus Cluster

Real pressure (sound) waves in the hot gas around the Perseus black hole, found in Chandra X-ray data and scaled up 57 and 58 octaves so humans can hear them. The radar sweep plays waves heading out in different directions.
